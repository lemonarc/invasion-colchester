<p>
    Invasion Colchester has welcomed an incredible line-up of actors, performers, artists, and behind-the-scenes talent over the years, with credits spanning the <strong>Star Wars</strong> saga, <strong>Doctor Who</strong>, <strong>Game of Thrones</strong>, the <strong>Marvel Cinematic Universe</strong>, <strong>Harry Potter</strong>, and many more.
</p>
<p>
    For 2026, we’re delighted to welcome performers <strong>Clem So</strong>, <strong>Ross Mullan</strong>, <strong>John Simpkin</strong>, <strong>Ross Sambridge</strong>, and <strong>James Taylor</strong>.
</p>
<p>
    Joining our screen guests this year are acclaimed movie sculptors <strong>Gary Pollard</strong> and <strong>Steve Braund</strong>, and guest artist <strong>Keith Burns</strong>. With acclaimed mixed-media artist <strong>Rob Bishop</strong> making an appearance at <strong>MP Galleries</strong>, we're bringing even more behind-the-scenes creativity to Invasion Colchester.
</p>
<p>
    From iconic creatures and background characters to stunt work, stand-ins, and unforgettable appearances across film,television, and other media, this year’s guests have helped bring some of the world’s biggest universes to life.
</p>

    <h3>Clem So</h3>
    <p>
        <strong>Clem So</strong> is a prolific background and character actor whose distinctive look has made him a recognisable figure across some of the biggest film franchises. His screen journey began with <strong>47 Ronin</strong>, and he quickly went on to land featured roles in acclaimed shows such as <strong>Sherlock</strong>, <strong>Being Human</strong>, and <strong>Doctor Who</strong>. In the latter, he appeared in multiple episodes — including as the <strong>Mandarin Clockwork Droid</strong> in "Deep Breath", a <strong>Space Corpse</strong> in "Oxygen", and a <strong>Ji-Hun Pirate</strong> in "Legend of the Sea Devils".
    </p>
    <p>
        Clem’s film credits span a wide range of high-profile titles, including <strong>Guardians of the Galaxy</strong>, <strong>Doctor Strange</strong>, <strong>Black Widow</strong>, <strong>Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness</strong>, <strong>Mission: Impossible – Dead Reckoning Part One</strong>, <strong>Morbius</strong>, and <strong>Ready Player One</strong>. He has appeared in both <strong>Star Wars: The Force Awakens</strong> and <strong>The Last Jedi</strong>, as well as <strong>Solo: A Star Wars Story</strong>, <strong>Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald</strong>, <strong>Jurassic World: Fallen Kingdom</strong>, and <strong>Zack Snyder’s Justice League</strong>.
    </p>
    <p>
        You might also have spotted Clem as part of King Randor's court in 2026's <strong>Masters of the Universe</strong>!

  <h3>Ross Mullan</h3>
  <p>
    <strong>Ross Mullan</strong> is a Canadian–British actor, creature performer, and puppeteer, best known as the
    original <strong>White Walker</strong> in HBO’s <strong>Game of Thrones</strong> (Seasons 2–4). On
    <strong>Doctor Who</strong> he embodied the terrifying <strong>The Silence</strong> and <strong>The Teller</strong>,
    cementing his reputation for unforgettable full-prosthetic roles.
  </p>
  <p>
    On the big screen, Mullan played one of the three <strong>Stygian Witches</strong> in Warner Bros.’
    <strong>Clash of the Titans</strong>, the ill-fated <strong>Dr. Wobegone</strong> in
    <strong>Sharknado 5: Global Swarming</strong>, and the fastidious <strong>Maître D'</strong> in Marvel’s
    <strong>Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania</strong>.
  </p>
  <p>
    For younger audiences he’s instantly recognisable as the enigmatic <strong>Numbertaker</strong> in the BBC’s
    <strong>Numberjacks</strong>. He also puppeteered and voiced <strong>Nev the Bear</strong> on
    <strong>Bear Behaving Badly</strong>, showcasing range beyond creature suits.
  </p>
  <p>
    Across film, television, commercials, and games, Mullan has portrayed countless werewolves, demons, and aliens, and
    contributed motion-capture and voice work to multiple titles and documentaries.

    <h3>Ross Sambridge</h3>
    <p>

    <h3>James Taylor</h3>
    <p>

<h4>Guest Movie Sculptors</h4>
<div class="container">
    <img src="/images/guests/gary-pollard-1.jpg" alt="Gary Pollard" class="guest-feature" />
    <h3>Gary Pollard</h3>
    <p>
        <strong>Gary Pollard</strong> is a sculptor, hands-on art director, and creature effects designer whose career has spanned more than four decades across film, television, theatre, themed environments, and specialist creative projects.
    </p>
    <p>
        After graduating from <strong>Nottingham Trent University</strong> in 1983 with a <strong>BA (Hons) degree in 3D Design (Theatre)</strong>, Gary secured his first professional engagement on Jim Henson’s fantasy classic <strong>Labyrinth</strong>. The production marked the beginning of a long career creating characters, creatures, props, and environments for the entertainment industry.
    </p>
    <p>
        Since then, Gary has contributed his sculpting and design skills to numerous screen and stage productions, combining traditional craftsmanship with practical creature-effects work. His experience includes detailed sculptural work, hands-on art direction, and the creation of imaginative characters and environments for a wide variety of productions and special projects.
    </p>

    <h3>Steve Braund</h3>
    <p>
        Steve Braund is the founder and sculptor behind <strong>Mind Magic Studios</strong>, a UK-based special effects studio specialising in hyper-realistic silicone and resin masks, prosthetics, costumes and props for film, television, theatre, live events and collectors.
    </p><p>
        Founded in 2014, Mind Magic Studios combines traditional sculpting techniques with modern production methods to create hand-crafted creature effects, character masks and bespoke props. Every piece is designed, sculpted, moulded, cast and finished by hand, earning the studio a reputation for exceptional realism, durability and attention to detail.
    </p><p>
        Steve trained with some of the UK's and Hollywood's leading creature sculptors at Gorton Studios, including acclaimed artist Don Lanning. His work has since been commissioned by filmmakers, theatre companies, scare attractions, advertising agencies, musicians, cosplayers, LARP groups and collectors around the world.
    </p><p>
        His film credits include the award-winning <em>Fluxx</em> and <em>ManFish</em>, where he created the lead amphibious creature, as well as <em>Member's Club</em>, <em>Bambi: The Reckoning</em>, and several upcoming productions, including work for a major fantasy streaming series. His creations have also appeared in advertising campaigns, themed attractions and live performances.
    </p><p>
        As both a sculptor and experienced costumer, Steve understands the importance of creating pieces that not only look incredible on screen but are also comfortable, expressive and practical to wear. Whether producing a screen-ready creature, a faithful replica or an original character design, his passion is bringing imagination to life through traditional craftsmanship and practical effects.
    </p>

<h4>Guest Artist</h4>

<div class="container">
    <img src="/images/guests/keith-burns-1.jpg" alt="Keith Burns" class="guest-feature" />
    <h3>Keith Burns</h3>
    <p>
        Keith Burns is an award-winning aviation artist and commercial illustrator whose work has earned international recognition for its historical accuracy, dramatic storytelling and meticulous attention to detail. With a career spanning more than two decades, Keith has become one of the UK's leading aviation artists, producing paintings, book illustrations and comic artwork for publishers and collectors around the world.
    </p><p>
        Well known to comic fans, Keith has spent almost 20 years illustrating comics, particularly acclaimed Second World War stories written by Garth Ennis. His distinctive artwork has brought numerous wartime tales to life, combining authentic aircraft, military history and cinematic action.
    </p><p>
        A full member of the Guild of Aviation Artists, Keith has also served on the judging panel for the Guild's prestigious annual exhibition. His work has received numerous accolades, including the Messier Dowty Award for Best Acrylic Painting in Show in 2015, the Aviation Painting of the Year Award in both 2016 and 2025, and the Patricia Forrest and the Late John Moore Best Group of Paintings Award in 2026.
    </p><p>
        Beyond comics and gallery work, Keith has illustrated postage stamps for Jersey Post, created artwork for twelve Ladybird books covering the Second World War, and has worked extensively with Aces High Gallery, producing original aviation paintings for collectors across the globe. He has also recently completed the artwork for his 50th <em>Commando</em> comic cover, marking a significant milestone in an already distinguished career.
    </p><p>
        Whether depicting iconic aircraft in flight or capturing pivotal moments in military history, Keith's artwork combines technical precision with a passion for aviation that has made him one of the most respected artists working in the genre today.
    </p>
